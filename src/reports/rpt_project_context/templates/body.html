Slope within each DGO is calculated by extracting the elevation at which the channel (NHD flowline) intersects the DGO at the upstream end and the downstream end from a 10 m DEM, and dividing the difference in elevation by the channel length. The average slope for the reach (all DGOs) is calculated by taking the minimum elevation within the upstream-most DGO, subtracting the minimum elevation within the downstream-most DGO and dividing by the channel length summed up across all DGOs.

The confinement ratio is calculated within each DGO by intersecting a buffered channel with the DGO polygons, which represent the valley bottom extent. Where these layers overlap indicates a confinging margin, and the length of confining margins is summed up and divided by the total channel length within the DGO.

Condition

The following figures present the data used to inform the information on riverscape condition, with a brief description of how each metric is calculated.

Low-lying valley bottom areas are estimated in VBET based on the topographic evidence raster, in which cells represent the relative likelihood of being valley bottom (0-1). These values are a function of both slope, and height above the channel. For low-lying valley bottom, this raster is thresholded at a higher value than the overall valley bottom, so this layer represents the areas that are lower relative to the channel, flatter, and adjacent to the channel, and therefore more likely to experience regular flooding.

Elevated valley bottom areas are estimated in VBET based on the topographic evidence raster, in which cells represent the relative likelihood of being valley bottom (0-1). These values are a function of both slope, and height above the channel. Elevated valley bottom areas are the areas that are part of the valley bottom but are topographically higher relative to the channel than the low-lying areas, not immediately adjacent to the channel, and therefore less likely to be active for flood regularly.

The proportion of riparian area within the valley bottom is calculated by summing up the area of pixels classified as riparian in the Landfire Existing Vegetation Type (EVT) dataset and dividing by the area of the DGO.

Floodplain accessibility represents the proportion of the valley bottom that a stream could theoretically access via geomorphic (e.g., channel migration) or hydrologic (e.g., overbank flows) processes. It is calculated by taking each pixel in a DEM within the area classified as valley bottom and tracing flow directions downstream. If an infrastructure layer (road, railroad, canal) is intersected prior to reaching the channel, all pixels along that path are classified as inaccessible and given a value of 0. If the path reaches the channel withough intersecting an infrastructure layer, all pixels along that path are classified as accessible and given a value of 1. The average of the 0s and 1s is calculated within each DGO to determine the overall floodplain accessibility.

The proportion of agricultural and developed land within the valley bottom is calculated by summing up the area of pixels classified as agricultural or developed in the Landfire Existing Vegetation Type (EVT) dataset and dividing by the area of the DGO.

Road and railroad density within the valley bottom is calculated by summing the length of roads and railroads and dividing by the centerline length of the DGO. Values greater than 1, therefore, indicate the length of the infrastructure is greater than the length of riverscape.

Beaver dam capacity is estimated using the BRAT model. BRAT estimates capacity as a function of the suitiabiliy of streamside vegetation as dam building material, stream power at base flow and typical flood flows, and channel slope.

Appendix 2 - Source Data

This report was generated using data from the Riverscapes Data Exchange. Free, publicly accessible data were processed through a series of models by the Riverscapes Consortium, culminating with the Riverscapes Metric Engine (RME) that synthesizes metrics together for all the preceding models. These RME data are based on polygons that span the valley bottom and are segmented at a distance that varies with drainage area. These polygons, or Discrete Geographic Objects (DGOs) possess over 100 metrics spanning a variety of topics pertaining to riverscapes health.

The model runs in question were all part of an initiative to produce a continuous riverscapes dataset for the Conterminous United States in 2025.
