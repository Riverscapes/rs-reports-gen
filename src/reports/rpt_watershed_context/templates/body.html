The Watershed Context Report summarizes key information about the watershed area provided as an input, as well as information for the entire HUC10 watersheds that intersect the area of interest. This helps provide context regarding the hydrologic, geomorphic, and ecological processes that may be operating in the watershed upstream of a reach of interest, and consequently impacting the form and behavior of that reach. It also may provide information regarding impacts within the watershed that can be important considerations for how a reach within the watershed may be affected, or respond to restoration interventions.

Geology, Ecoregions, and Ownership

Geology information is generated by intersecting the input area of interest (watershed) with the State Geologic Map Compilation (SGMC) dataset.

Ecoregions

Ecoregion information is generated by intersecting the input area of interest (watershed) with the EPA Level IV Ecoregions dataset.

Ownership

Ownership information is generated by intersecting the input area of interest (watershed) with the BLM Surface Management Agency (SMA) dataset.

Landcover

Landcover information comes from the 'Physiognomy' field in the Landfire Existing Vegetation Type (EVT) dataset. The total number of pixels of each cateogrical type is summed up within the HUC10 watersheds that intersect the area of interest.

Climate

Data are derived from PRISM 30 year normal precipitation raster dataset with 800 meter spatial resolution. These statistics illustrate the range of average annual precipitation values over the spatial extent of the subject area of interest.

Appendices

Appendix 1 - Source Data

This report was generated using data from the Riverscapes Data Exchange. Free, publicly accessible data were processed through a series of models by the Riverscapes Consortium, culminating with the Riverscapes Metric Engine (RME) that synthesizes metrics together for all the preceding models. These RME data are based on polygons that span the valley bottom and are segmented at a distance that varies with drainage area. These polygons, or Discrete Geographic Objects (DGOs) possess over 100 metrics spanning a variety of topics pertaining to riverscapes health.

The model runs in question were all part of an initiative to produce a continuous riverscapes dataset for the Conterminous United States in 2025.
