Summary of Streams, Riparian-Wetland Areas, and Waterbodies

Hydrographic features are the surficial expressions and/or conduits of water, sediment, and organic matter in a watershed that support most riparian-wetland and aquatic areas. Here, we summarize the location, type, extent, ownership patterns, and key attributes of the hydrographic features and associated riparian-wetland and aquatic resources. These details establish the spatial domain and context for assessing resource conditions, trends, contributing factors, monitoring needs, and management options.

Characteristics of Hydrographic Features & Riparian-Wetland Areas

This section summarizes key biophysical contextual attributes that commonly influence formative processes, biophysical attributes, and/or resource values of: (i) streams/rivers, (ii) riparian-wetland areas, and (iii) waterbodies, seeps and springs. This information helps analysts:

  • interpret the relevance and overall significance of various metrics and indicators to an area
  • identify types of areas that provide specific values of interest
  • Estimate the sensitivity of features to various managment actions
  • target the location and types of management interventions that would elicit desired outcomes most efficiently and effectively

Streams and Rivers

Streams by Type

Why this is useful: Stream types dictate the hydrologic regime and types of riparian vegetation that can be supported

Streams by Strahler Stream Order

Why this is useful: Strahler Stream Order measures relative stream size. Higher-order streams are larger and integrate cumulative upstream processes.

Channel Slope: Perennial Streams

Why this is useful: Channel slope influences stream power, sediment transport, and habitat potential. Perennial streams often have lower slopes in valley bottoms.

Channel Slope: Non-Perennial Streams

Why this is useful: Channel slope influences stream power, sediment transport, and habitat potential. Non-perennial streams often have steeper slopes and more variable flow regimes.

Streams by Valley Confinement

Why this is useful: confinement describes lateral restriction by valley walls. Unconfined channels have greater potential for restoration and habitat complexity.

Riparian-Wetland Areas

Riparian-Wetland Areas

Why this is useful: Riparian and wetland areas are the most productive habitats in arid landscapes. BLM is required to manage these areas for Proper Functioning Condition (PFC) to protect biodiversity, water quality, and forage.
